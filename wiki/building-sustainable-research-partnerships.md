# Building Sustainable Research Partnerships

## Why this matters

Research collaboration units (biostatistics cores, data science teams, consulting groups) often fail not because of bad science, but because of unclear expectations, unsustainable funding, or partnerships that don't match the team's actual mission. This article lays out practical guidance for building partnerships that last.

## The six-rule framework

1. **Align with institutional strategic plans** — Before saying yes to a new collaboration, check whether it supports your institution's broader goals, not just one department's immediate need.
2. **Cultivate partnerships that fit your mission** — Say no to work that's outside your team's actual expertise or purpose, even if it seems useful in the moment.
3. **Ensure sustainability and prepare for growth** — Don't build your team around one grant or one contract. Plan for what happens when funding changes.
4. **Define clear expectations in a written agreement** — Put scope, roles, cost, timeline, and evaluation criteria in writing. Verbal understandings fall apart.
5. **Communicate regularly** — Set up recurring check-ins rather than waiting for problems to surface.
6. **Expect the unexpected** — Build in flexibility for staff turnover, funding shifts, or scope changes.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Does this partnership support a real institutional priority?
- [ ] Does it match our team's actual mission and expertise?
- [ ] Do we have a plan if funding changes next year?
- [ ] Is there a written agreement covering scope, cost, and duration?
- [ ] Do we have a recurring communication plan (not just ad hoc emails)?
- [ ] Have we planned for what happens if a key person leaves?

## Common mistakes

- Agreeing to informal, undocumented collaborations "just to help out"
- Taking on work outside the team's core mission because it's easy to say yes
- Assuming a partnership will renew itself without a written agreement
- Treating communication as optional once a project is underway
