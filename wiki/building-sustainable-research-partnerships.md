# Building and Running Sustainable Research Partnerships

## Why this matters

Research collaboration units (biostatistics cores, data science teams, consulting groups) often fail not because of bad science, but because of unclear expectations, unsustainable funding, or partnerships that quietly fall apart after the excitement of the launch wears off. This article covers the full lifecycle: how to responsibly start a partnership, and how to keep it healthy once it's running.

## Part 1: Building the partnership (starting well)

1. **Align with institutional strategic plans** — Before saying yes to a new collaboration, check whether it supports your institution's broader goals, not just one department's immediate need.
2. **Cultivate partnerships that fit your mission** — Say no to work that's outside your team's actual expertise or purpose, even if it seems useful in the moment.
3. **Ensure sustainability and prepare for growth** — Don't build your team around one grant or one contract. Plan for what happens when funding changes.
4. **Define clear expectations in a written agreement** — Put scope, roles, cost, timeline, and evaluation criteria in writing. Verbal understandings fall apart.
5. **Communicate regularly** — Set up recurring check-ins rather than waiting for problems to surface.
6. **Expect the unexpected** — Build in flexibility for staff turnover, funding shifts, or scope changes.

## Part 2: Running the partnership (staying healthy)

Once a partnership is underway, it moves through three ongoing phases:

1. **Initiation** — Even after the paperwork is signed, confirm that everyone agrees on why the partnership exists, who the stakeholders are, and what success looks like.
2. **Maintenance / Management** — This is the daily/weekly work: track project status, communicate proactively, and resolve small conflicts before they grow. Trust and transparency are maintained here, not assumed.
3. **Evaluation / Sustainability** — On a regular cadence (e.g. annually), step back and honestly ask: is this partnership working? Document lessons learned, and decide whether to continue, adjust, or expand it.

## Practical checklist

**Before starting:**
- [ ] Does this partnership support a real institutional priority?
- [ ] Does it match our team's actual mission and expertise?
- [ ] Do we have a plan if funding changes next year?
- [ ] Is there a written agreement covering scope, cost, and duration?

**While running it:**
- [ ] Do we have a recurring communication plan (not just ad hoc emails)?
- [ ] Is someone actively tracking project status and effort?
- [ ] Are small disagreements addressed early rather than left to build up?

**Periodically:**
- [ ] Have we reviewed whether this partnership is still delivering value?
- [ ] Have we documented what we've learned so far?
- [ ] Have we decided, with evidence, whether to continue, adjust, or end it?

## Common mistakes

- Agreeing to informal, undocumented collaborations "just to help out"
- Taking on work outside the team's core mission because it's easy to say yes
- Assuming a partnership will run itself once the agreement is signed
- Treating communication and status tracking as optional once a project is underway
- Never revisiting whether an old partnership is still worth continuing
